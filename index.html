A management sim about the AI boom

Build the AI boom.
Then build what comes after it.

You start in 2027 with a garage, a seed round and a tiny model called Bit. Rent compute, pour concrete, order chips that sail through real chokepoints and keep your models safe. Grow into a hyperscaler, then into the steward of a planet-wide project — and climb from Kardashev 0.73 to Type I. Every place you build is a living 3D scale model.

Real constraints, readable at a glance

A data hall really takes the best part of a year, a fab really needs ultrapure water, and the grid queue is real. You watch it rise on site; icons up front, the real units a hover away.

Geography matters

Sun in the Sahara, cold in the Nordics, fabs on the Pacific rim. Your chips sail through Malacca, Suez and Panama — and reroute when a strait closes.

Crises are puzzles

Quakes, export controls, heatwaves, memory crunches and AI incidents. Every card is a trade-off, and your advisors don’t always agree.

Safety is the job

When capability outruns alignment, incidents follow. Safety research also earns trust, permits — and the right to hand the wheel to an AI.

Something always grows

Exponential compute, learning-curve costs, offline progress and goals with progress bars. Coming back feels good.

Charm, at every scale

Zoom into any site: people keep a working day, crews hammer on the building site, trucks queue at the junction and the lights come on at dusk — and advisors with opinions age with you across seventy years.

The score

What is the Kardashev scale?

In 1964 astronomer Nikolai Kardashev ranked civilisations by the power they command. Carl Sagan made it continuous:

K = (log₁₀ P − 6) / 10

Humanity runs on about 19.6 terawatts — K 0.729. A Type I civilisation harnesses 10 petawatts, around five hundred times more. AI is how you climb; energy is how you’re measured. Your score is the year you get there.

“Unknown publisher”? How to open it

The builds aren’t code-signed yet, so your computer will ask before running them.

  • Windows: on the SmartScreen notice, click More info → Run anyway.
  • macOS: right-click the app → Open, or System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway.
  • Linux: make the AppImage executable (chmod +x) and run it (Arch-based distros may need the fuse2 package), or install the .deb on Debian and Ubuntu.
  • Android: open the downloaded .apk and, if asked, allow your browser to install apps.
