# Changelog

## 0.15.2 — Way In (2026-11-14)
The last of the realism pass's leftovers, and everyone who works on a campus now goes in at a door.

- **People go in at the door** of every power plant and factory of the scale-up: the SMR plant, the reactor restart, the nuclear plant, hydro, geothermal, the packaging plant, the fab and the gigawatt campus. Their staff used to walk to the edge of the plot and vanish on the open ground in front of it. Now each plant's entrance opens for them, a paved way leads to it from the street, and they walk round the buildings, tanks and fences on the plot instead of through them.
- **And everywhere else:** the suppliers' fabs, offices, warehouses and gas yards, the mines, the cable landing, the Charter's buildings in Geneva (up their steps), the launch sites and the megaprojects (their offices, control buildings, substations, gatehouses and every fusion plant's control block). Yards, fields and building sites, where nobody would go indoors, no longer have staff vanishing at their edge.
- **Pedestrian gates** in the fences of the fusion plants, the converter station and the tank farm, where the office door stood a step behind the fence.
- **The gas plant's operators** walk onto the platform and in at a door instead of vanishing at the yard gate. The research lab's front door opens for its staff (it never did).
- **Nobody sinks into the paving** or floats beside it: people walk on forecourts, steps, podiums, platforms and the hydro road at their real height, and round shrub beds instead of through them.
- **A car no longer parks across a door:** the SMR plant's last bay ran through its lobby, and the geothermal plant's stood in front of its control building's door. The gas yard's tube trailer no longer fills its gateway.
- **A half-built wall has a top:** while a building goes up, its walls show their thickness at the line they have reached, with the bare inside beyond, instead of a paper-thin shell. The data hall's cladding ribs no longer show through on the inside.
- **The gas plant's control house** no longer has the old hut standing inside it (it showed through the walls while they went up).
- **Aurora is lighter to draw:** its far trees are the simpler kind, as on every campus's horizon, a patch at a time as you move about (a quarter fewer triangles in the habitat, the same picture).

## 0.15.1 — Loose Ends (2026-11-14)
What the realism pass saw and left, finished. (0.15.0 was never released on its own: this update brings both.)

- **Wheels turn.** Every car, van and truck rolls on wheels that go round, at the speed it is driving.
- **The flatbed comes in colours** like the other vehicles. Only its cab takes the paint: the timber deck and the load's straps stay as they are.
- **Buildings go up at the pace of the work.** A plant used to have its low buildings finished in the first quarter of the build and then nothing but one stack climbing for the rest. Now the foundations and the halls take most of the time and the tall parts follow.
- **An expansion looks like one.** Upgrading a building used to show the finished bigger one from the first day. Now the old building stands in scaffolding while the new one rises through it from the ground; solar fields, battery yards and wind farms gain their new rows a few at a time.
- **A reactor restart is an old plant put back to work:** it stands there whole from the first day, cold, with a crane over it, and steams when it comes online. It used to be "built" from the ground like a new one.
- **Parked cars arrive when a building opens.** They used to grow out of the car park slice by slice with the walls.
- **The concrete mixer no longer parks inside the entrance** of a building that fills its plot.
- **People's feet:** nobody floats beside a porch or a step any more (the map of what can be stood on is twice as fine). Nobody walks into the garage's open bay, through the workbench, and vanishes: the front door is the way in.
- **On the Moon, crews lope:** long, slow, low strides, leaning into them.
- **Street lamps** are a tapered column with a swept arm and a lantern, not a square post with a square bar.
- **The Steppe Cosmodrome is inland:** no sea behind its pad, and no surf to hear.
- **Doors open inwards.** A door swinging out could sweep through whoever was arriving at the step.
- **Front wheels steer** into a bend, and wheels turn in a moving vehicle's shadow too.
- **A new concrete mixer** with glass in its windows (the old one had open holes), in the same paints as the other trucks.
- **A half-built wall** shows bare concrete inside instead of its finished paint.
- **The open pit's trucks back up to a tip wall** and tip over it onto the stockpile. They used to tip driving forwards, their tails in the pile.
- **The reactor and the restart are floodlit at night:** a soft wash up the cooling towers and the containment, lamps round the dome. They were black shapes after dark.
- **The gas plant's control house** is a building at human scale, with a real door, a control room over the pipe yard and a lit window.
- **Hydro:** no more hard white glare across the reservoir; the tailrace ends in outlet works under the road instead of stopping at the edge of the plot.
- **Building sites of the megaprojects are lit at night:** floodlights on the tower cranes, lit builders' boards. Pumped hydro is built in a works yard round a clean excavation; the laser fusion plant goes up where the finished one will stand; trucks and crews at the converter station no longer stand inside its plant.
- **Geneva's flags** carry drawn emblems on clean fields instead of blocky squares.
- **Aurora:** cherry trees in blossom, tall cypresses and flower beds with real flowers, in place of the last old-style shapes.
- **Suppliers' sites** glow at night no brighter than your own plants.
- **Lit at night:** the research lab's own windows, pane by pane, with lights under its canopy; the gas plant gets a row of windows under each hall's eaves, lights over its doors and along its walkways, and a lit control house. Signs on the SMR plant and the fab are lit after dark.
- **The plants of the scale-up, looked at closely:**
  - Hydro: foam at the outlets that fades at its edges instead of hard white discs; the service truck parks beside the road, not across it.
  - Geothermal, reactor, gigawatt campus, packaging, fab: lamps that stood inside buildings, cooling towers, trailers or a row of generators stand where a lamp would.
  - Restart and reactor: the office stands in the fence line with its door on the street (the fence ran through it); the restart's skip is an open skip of rubble and its site cabin is tall enough to stand in.
  - Fab: the gate is where the road is, with a real barrier; the pipe bridge spans the lane instead of standing in it.
  - Gigawatt campus: cooling water runs in pipes overhead, not in boxes across the street; a flag that waves.
  - Packaging: the lorries' cabs no longer stand on the grass past the plot.
- **Suppliers' sites:** real transformers in place of olive boxes; lorries and vans stand on their docks, not in them, and are away at night; a proper wheel loader at the brine ponds; the warehouse's name is readable; the pit office has a door.
- **Megaprojects under construction:**
  - Each fusion design goes up as itself (a capsule on cradles, a stellarator inside rising portal frames, a laser chamber closing on its pedestal, a pinch vessel under its gantry). All five used to be a torus in a tokamak's drum.
  - Pumped hydro is built at its real size, its lower basin a dry excavation until the works are done.
  - The project office's roof is a planted meadow.
- **Asteroids:** ore rides in lidded tubs on a hauled cable between closed houses (it lay loose on an open belt, in next to no gravity); the hauler's load is netted down; crews glide along guide lines, clipped on, instead of walking; whoever stands still is tethered.
- **The yards in orbit:** every finished piece leaves under tow, none drifts off loose. Spacewalkers are tethered.
- **Geneva:** the secretariat's pool and the plaza's fountain hold real water over a tiled floor, under a stone coping.
- **Aurora:** hedges, flower beds and green roofs are real planting; ponds, pools and the fountain hold water; children play beside the swings and the slide, not inside them; people walk round the beds, pools and café tables instead of through them.

## 0.15.0 — True to Life (2026-11-14)
A pass over the 3D world for everything that would not be so in real life.

- **Cars have glass.** Every vehicle's windows are dark glass that mirrors the sky and catches the sun. The red hatchback with red windows is replaced by a new one, and the semi and the flatbed are new models too.
- **Cars come in colours.** A car park is no longer a row of the same red car: each car gets its own paint (white, silver, grey, black, blue, red, green, mustard and more), and no two side by side match.
- **Lamps sit on the bodywork.** Tail lights were pale balls hanging in the air behind trucks. They are red lenses on each vehicle's own rear now, headlights likewise.
- **The street ends properly.** The grey disc at the end of a campus road is a real turning circle: the road flares into it, the edge line runs all the way round, there is a planted island in the middle and a striped barrier where the road will go on when the campus grows. Deliveries drive round the island.
- **Roads look like roads:** sealed cracks, a ragged gravel edge where the asphalt meets the verge.
- **Trees, rebuilt.** Every tree, bush and cactus is new: real trunks and limbs, crowns of leaves instead of blobs and cones, several shapes of each kind, pines with drooping boughs, palms with fronds, birches, acacias, mesquites, sagebrush in dry country. They sway a little in the wind (not with Reduce motion on). Far-off trees are simpler ones of the same shape and colour. They also cost less to draw than the old ones.
- **Doors open.** People used to walk into a shut door and vanish. A door now swings open as someone comes up to it, they walk through into the dark, and it closes behind them.
- **Traffic waits for people** crossing a campus road.
- **Nobody walks through a lamp post, a power pole or a street tree.**
- **The grid substation has a yard:** crushed stone, a chain-link fence with warning plates, a gate and an apron onto the road. It used to stand on bare grass.
- **The ranch:** real cattle (the box cows are gone) at a pond with a trodden bank, reeds and rippling water, on open range instead of in a ploughed field.
- **Trees along the access road** and in the turning circle's island, of the country the campus is in. No tree stands right in front of the camera in the low views any more.
- **The buildings you build, looked at one by one:**
  - Gas plant: a way up onto its deck, a gas metering skid, transformers, lamps (it was black at night), the red warning lights on the stack rims instead of floating over a roof. The bigger plants are real combined-cycle blocks now: boilers behind the halls, a turbine house, a cooling tower.
  - Research lab: wings with windows, doors and roof plant instead of blank boxes, lit at night; the car park's bays sit between the lines; the biggest lab no longer hangs off the back of its plot.
  - Solar farm, battery yard, wind farm: rebuilt. Battery cabinets are taller than a person now, wind towers go up in sections while they are built, the solar field has a service lane and an inverter station inside its plot.
  - The garage: a real picket fence, a letter box, lit windows at night, a porch light, proper bushes and a leafy tree.
  - Open pit: the shovel digs where you can see it. Reactor restart: the conductors no longer run across the roller door. Lights for the fab, the crate yard and the crusher at night.
- **The later places:**
  - Launch pad: nothing fades in any more. The booster comes back down and lands on its mount, a transporter brings the next upper stack, the tower's crane lifts it on, and it launches.
  - North Sea wind farm at night: a lamp on every foundation, crew boats with lit wheelhouses and navigation lights.
  - Pumped hydro: the back of the mountain is a forested slope, not a wall; the stream has a bed.
  - Geneva: people on benches and at the café are figures, not blocks; nobody walks through a canopy post, a planter or the kiosk.
  - Asteroids: no smoke rising in a vacuum, finer dust, and the dark ones are no longer near-black.
  - Aurora's trees stay off the window frame. Cacti grow only in American deserts (the Sahara had saguaros).
- **Small things:** a crane's warning light turns with its jib; boulders on green land are grey stone, not orange; scaffolding stops at a sensible height; people at the edge of a porch stand on it, not in it.

## 0.14.2 — Clear View (2026-11-14)
The square that flickered on the globe is gone, along with a run of small bugs.

- **Fixed: a square that strobed on the globe** in the Linux app, in the main menu and in game, while the camera moved or zoomed. Two of the globe's effects, the sea's reflection of the sky and the aurora seen edge-on, could make a broken pixel on some graphics engines, and the glow spread it into a flashing square. Both are fixed where they start, along with the same kind of mistake in the clouds, the cable lines, the campus skies and the shading. The glow now drops a broken pixel instead of spreading it.
- **Keyboard shortcuts:**
  - Ctrl+C and other Ctrl, Cmd and Alt combinations no longer open panels (Ctrl+C opened Compute).
  - Holding a key no longer flickers a panel open and shut; only panning and zooming repeat.
  - After dragging a slider, Space, 1–4 and the panel keys work straight away, without a click elsewhere first. The slider keeps its arrow keys.
  - Escape leaves a text field and closes New company or Import.
  - During the Type I ending only Escape does anything. M used to swap the globe in under it, and the ending lost its music, its last shot and the end card's picture.
- **Skip ahead:**
  - It ran at half speed while the mouse was still.
  - It kept going under a briefing, then started the clock behind it. It now stops for one and can't start while one is open.
  - With Pause for decisions off, a decision already waiting made every skip stop after one day.
- **Double clicks:** Update could start two downloads, Found the company and Import could each make two saves, and a double click on a decision could answer the next card too.
- **On the globe, the Charter HQ, the megaprojects and the launch sites** said "Logic fabs" when you pointed at them. They now say where they are and what they are.
- **Settings that do what they say:**
  - Reduce motion now stills the campus clouds and leaves out rain, snow and dust.
  - Turning Weather on or off applies at once on a campus.
  - Battery saver's description says what it does: it draws about 30 frames a second instead of 60.
  - Reset settings no longer forgets the interface looks you have reached.
- **Small things:** no "running slowly… about 0 frames a second" question when the game starts in a window you can't see; the 3D view stays sharp when the window moves to a screen with a different scale; a contract you were halfway through posting in the Charter no longer carries over into another save.

## 0.14.0 — Mission Control (2026-11-13)
The interface now grows up with your company: plywood and paper in the garage, pure light by Type I.

- **An interface that changes with every act.** Nothing moves (panels down the left, the clock and the figures across the top, the Kardashev scale at the right, the site you picked on the right, what is coming along the bottom), but what it is made of does.
  - **Act I, the garage:** plank rails, figures on pinned paper tags, the date on an index card, label-maker keys, a tape measure for the Kardashev scale, sheets held up with masking tape, goals on a sticky note.
  - **Act II, the scale-up:** metal rails, amber readouts, lit keys, a brass gauge, paper on clipboards.
  - **Act III, the Charter:** slabs of black glass, touch keys, the site on a tablet, notes written with a stylus. The sticky notes are still stuck on the hardware.
  - **Act IV, planetary:** one thin strip of metal left along the top; everything else is projected in teal light.
  - **Act V, Type I:** only light. The Kardashev scale is an arc across the sky with the Sun where you stand. One thing is solid: Juno's sticky note from 2027, kept.
- **Notes on the world.** On a campus, what the interface has to say about a building is pinned to the building: "nearly full!", "empty! order chips", "7 mo to go", "free plot". Point at a building and it is ringed, with its card beside it on a leader instead of chasing the pointer. Each act writes in its own hand: paper flags and a red pen, grease pencil on the glass, a stylus, then labels and brackets of light.
- **Decisions follow suit:** a newspaper clipping in the garage, a typed signal in Act II, a message on glass in Act III, a transmission in light after that.
- **The title screen and the loading screen wear the look of the company you would continue** (the garage's when you have none).
- **Settings → Display → Interface look** keeps any look you have already reached, if you like one too much to leave it.
- **Coming up moved to the bottom of the screen,** beside the latest news: a line of what you are waiting for, soonest first. Click one to skip the clock ahead to it. Acts IV and V show what is on the horizon there (the plan's end, the next stage, projects, the Council).
- **The left rail's keys carry their names** when there is room for them.
- **Every panel opens with its numbers.** Orders, Stats, Goals, Team, Deals, Blocs, the Charter, the Council, the Planet and the System now lead with a row of big figures, as Compute, Finance and Research do.
- **Finishing an act** is a certificate with a gold seal in the paper acts.
- **High contrast** has its own rules in every look.

Under it, the first pass of the rebuild: a new typeface, panels that lead with their numbers, a build menu with pictures.

- **A display typeface.** Titles, labels, buttons and every figure are set in Saira, a squared, technical face; running text stays in Atkinson Hyperlegible. Numbers look the same in a sentence as in a table, and the slashed zeros are gone.
- **Night-blue glass.** Panes are deeper and bluer, with a sheen at the top, a crisp edge over a bright sky, brighter text, and clearer greens, ambers and reds.
- **The top bar:** the speed buttons and the date are one clock, with a light that runs along its foot while time does and an amber edge when it stops ("Paused" replaces the company's name). Each figure sits on a plate in its own colour. The Kardashev meter has a ring that fills toward the next milestone, and says what that milestone is.
- **The dock:** every button on its own plate, the panels in groups, and a label that stands beside the button you point at: its name, its shortcut key, and one line on what it is for.
- **Panels that lead with their numbers.** Compute, Finance and Research open with a row of big figures (compute, best model, revenue and safety margin; cash with its curve, net a month, runway and valuation; research a day, how much is done, what is in the lab). Compute shows your split as one coloured bar over its sliders and the safety margin on a dial; Finance draws the month's costs as shares; halves of the later acts' panels sit on cards.
- **Research** has a colour for each field and a count at the head of each column; finished projects fold down to their names, open ones show their cost on a tag, and the one in the lab glows.
- **A build menu with pictures.** Every building in the site panel is shown as its own 3D model, in the build cards and in the list of what stands there.
- **Event cards wear their kind's colour** (a disaster glows red-orange, politics and safety blue, the market green, the story gold), their picture stands on a lit plate, and each choice has an arrow that leans in when you point at it. Finishing an act gets a gold card.
- **The title screen:** the menu is in capitals without its small icons, in three groups; Continue says whose company it opens, which act it is in, the date, the Kardashev figure and how long you have played.
- **The loading screen** shows the planet's edge at sunrise under the logo, a line of progress with what is being done, and something to read: one of a dozen facts and tips.
- **Small things:** tooltips have a title line and show shortcut keys as keys; switches, sliders, tabs, segmented choices and progress bars are redrawn; the close button turns as it lights.

## 0.13.2 — Solid Ground (2026-11-11)
A sweep for things that looked wrong: on the campuses, on the globe, in space and in the interface.

- **Feet on the ground.** People stood ankle-deep in paving, porches and plots, and walked through each other. They now stand on whatever is under them and keep an arm's length apart; Bit floats clear of heads.
- **Cars on their surface.** Parked cars sank into some car parks and floated over others (a third of a metre at the chip fab), overlapped in their bays and, at the data halls, stood half a metre off their marks. Traffic sat in the road.
- **Car parks you can see.** The asphalt and bay lines of every data hall, lab and wind farm lay buried under the plot's paving, like the garage's driveway and path. Paved paths now go round car parks instead of under the cars, and lie flush with the ground.
- **Night:** the data hall's lobby window was a blank white board; it is framed glass now, lit from inside.
- **The gas plant:** nobody wades across its platform, and the cement mixer no longer parks inside it.
- **Roads:** Geneva's street, its pavement and its lamps no longer run into the lake; on the big megaproject sites and at the spaceport the access road no longer stops in open ground: it runs up past every row, with a gravel lane along each.
- **Skies:** Wyoming's buttes stood as a striped wall across the sky; the plains keep theirs low. Desert farm circles are worked in quarters with wheel tracks instead of one flat green. The sky over the asteroids and the yards has its stars back, stations in open space are no longer burnt out to white or lost in black, and Moon rocks are grey, not sandstone. Steam and smoke take the colour of the sunset instead of glowing white.
- **The globe:** labels keep clear of the interface and the screen's edge and fade in and out; megaprojects no longer sit on a khaki disc or scatter reactors in the sea, and they go dark at night like the land round them; solar fields have their true colour; power lines end at a station instead of at nothing; the first liftoff and the test satellite can actually be seen; freight grows as you come down to it, and a shipment crossing land is a lorry.
- **The menu film:** every globe shot has its own time of day, so "Night falls over Asia" is no longer in daylight when you open the game at the wrong hour.
- **Space:** the solar-system view's labels lost their faint boxes; power beams no longer swell into searchlights when the camera flies close.
- **No more emoji.** Event cards, the news, toasts, goals, reports and the handbook used about 130 different emoji as markers; they are drawn as the game's own icons now.
- **A top bar that fits.** In Acts IV and V the safety margin hid under the K meter; the bar now tightens itself step by step, and big numbers have thousands separators. The dock fits all 19 buttons on a small window.
- **Tidier panels:** one kind of scrollbar everywhere, rows that line up (grid routes, asteroids, orders, sliders), labels that no longer break after one word, the right icons on the Field Guide and Achievements windows, and panes solid enough that the map does not read through them.
- **Other places:** mine trucks no longer drive through each other, two wind turbines on one tile no longer cross blades, the launch pad's flame trench and crawlerway are visible, launch smoke takes the light of the hour, and Ring One's radiator no longer breaks into sparkles.

## 0.13.1 — Strong Suit (2026-11-11)
The stronger graphics card now really is the faster one on Linux.

- **Fixed: the game ran far slower on the stronger graphics card in the Linux app** (about 3 frames a second instead of 19 on the test laptop). The web engine packed inside the app could not pass pictures from that card to the screen without a slow detour. With Stronger chosen, the app now uses your system's own web engine when there is one: 36 frames a second on the same laptop.
- **The trial now measures.** After switching, the game times how fast it runs and shows you the number beside the automatic card's. If the stronger card is slower on your computer, it says so and suggests going back; a choice kept earlier that runs slowly gets the same question at the next start.
- The trial's countdown follows the clock even when the game is struggling, and lasts 25 seconds.

## 0.12.0 — All In (2026-11-11)
One game, in 3D, on your computer. The pixel-art edition and every way of playing outside the desktop app are retired, so all the work goes into the game that is heading for Steam.

- **3D is the game now.** The app opens straight into it: no Edition switch, no hand-over from the pixel game. Your companies, settings and achievements carry over untouched.
- **Desktop only: Windows, macOS and Linux.** The browser version, the installable web app and the Android app are retired. Old links to the web game lead to the home page.
- **Settings tidied.** Gone: Edition, Pixel size, Clouds on the map and Fullscreen on phones (none of them did anything in 3D). Soundtrack is a plain choice now: Cinematic (the default) or Chiptune.

- Kept from the pixel days, on purpose: the logo, the advisors' portraits, the small interface icons and the Type I end card are still pixel art.

## 0.11.0 — Golden Hour (2026-11-10)
The 3D edition, filmed properly: real skies and light at every hour, a living globe, a space worth looking at, an interface made for it and a new sound. The pixel edition looks as it did, and gets the new sound engine too.

**Light and sky (3D campuses)**
- A real sky over every campus: the sun and the moon as discs, drifting clouds lit from the sun's side, stars and the Milky Way at night, and the northern lights over Luleå, the fjords and Reykjanes.
- Light that follows the hour: crisp noon, a warm golden hour with long shadows, a pink sunset, a blue hour when the windows come on, and moonlit nights you can see by.
- Haze toward the horizon, warm when you look into the sun, so the far land fades into the sky instead of ending.
- Lamps really light the ground, the walls, the cars and the people near them at night, and headlights sweep along the road.
- Glass, solar panels and water mirror the sky.
- Sharper, deeper pictures: the blur now follows true distance (sharp where you look, soft in front and behind), with richer contrast and colour.

**Places**
- Tilt the camera all the way down to the horizon: mesas, mountain ranges, forests and the sea are part of the view now. Cliffs show their rock in level layers.
- Lakes and seas have turquoise shallows, a line of foam on the beach, ripples and a glint under the sun or the moon.
- Rain falls in fine streaks and wets the roads and roofs; under cloud the light goes soft and grey.
- Aurora, the habitat, is seen down its length through a wider lens: the land curving up both sides and overhead, the sky windows, the sun-line and the bulkhead at the far end.
- Arriving at a place, the picture fades up and the camera comes down and settles.

**The globe**
- The Earth rebuilt: real weather (the tropical rain belt, storm tracks, spiral storms, clear deserts) drifting over it and casting shadows, forests, deserts and snow in their own colours, mountains that catch the low sun, turquoise coasts, and the sun glinting on the sea.
- A proper atmosphere: a thin bright limb by day, orange and violet along the edge of night.
- The night side is alive: towns and cities as thousands of small lights, moonlit land and sea, auroras round both poles.
- Stars in their colours, the Milky Way, and the Sun itself in the sky.
- Cables, sea lanes and power lines glow, with light travelling along them; sites ping when you pick them; labels are easier to read.
- The globe now turns and zooms under your hand: the place under the cursor stays under the cursor.

**Space and the ending**
- The solar system view: a living Sun with a corona and flares, the swarm as thousands of sails that catch the light on their orbits, lit planets and asteroids, the Milky Way behind.
- The orbit view: the Earth fills the frame, satellites fly in their planes with trails, stations carry running lights, launches rise as arcs of fire, and a solar storm lights the auroras.
- Reaching Type I is now one unbroken shot: sunrise over the Earth's edge, out past the Moon, and the swarm turning to the Sun.

**The 3D interface**
