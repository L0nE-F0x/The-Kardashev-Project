# Changelog

## 0.12.0 — All In (2026-11-11)
One game, in 3D, on your computer. The pixel-art edition and every way of playing outside the desktop app are retired, so all the work goes into the game that is heading for Steam.

- **3D is the game now.** The app opens straight into it: no Edition switch, no hand-over from the pixel game. Your companies, settings and achievements carry over untouched.
- **Desktop only: Windows, macOS and Linux.** The browser version, the installable web app and the Android app are retired. Old links to the web game lead to the home page.
- **Settings tidied.** Gone: Edition, Pixel size, Clouds on the map and Fullscreen on phones (none of them did anything in 3D). Soundtrack is a plain choice now: Cinematic (the default) or Chiptune.
- **The website** shows the 3D Earth from the first moment (no pixel globe first), and offers one download per system, with yours picked out.
- **A lighter app.** The pixel renderer and its engine are no longer shipped or loaded.
- Kept from the pixel days, on purpose: the logo, the advisors' portraits, the small interface icons and the Type I end card are still pixel art.

## 0.11.0 — Golden Hour (2026-11-10)
The 3D edition, filmed properly: real skies and light at every hour, a living globe, a space worth looking at, an interface made for it and a new sound. The pixel edition looks as it did, and gets the new sound engine too.

**Light and sky (3D campuses)**
- A real sky over every campus: the sun and the moon as discs, drifting clouds lit from the sun's side, stars and the Milky Way at night, and the northern lights over Luleå, the fjords and Reykjanes.
- Light that follows the hour: crisp noon, a warm golden hour with long shadows, a pink sunset, a blue hour when the windows come on, and moonlit nights you can see by.
- Haze toward the horizon, warm when you look into the sun, so the far land fades into the sky instead of ending.
- Lamps really light the ground, the walls, the cars and the people near them at night, and headlights sweep along the road.
- Glass, solar panels and water mirror the sky.
- Sharper, deeper pictures: the blur now follows true distance (sharp where you look, soft in front and behind), with richer contrast and colour.

**Places**
- Tilt the camera all the way down to the horizon: mesas, mountain ranges, forests and the sea are part of the view now. Cliffs show their rock in level layers.
- Lakes and seas have turquoise shallows, a line of foam on the beach, ripples and a glint under the sun or the moon.
- Rain falls in fine streaks and wets the roads and roofs; under cloud the light goes soft and grey.
- Aurora, the habitat, is seen down its length through a wider lens: the land curving up both sides and overhead, the sky windows, the sun-line and the bulkhead at the far end.
- Arriving at a place, the picture fades up and the camera comes down and settles.

**The globe**
- The Earth rebuilt: real weather (the tropical rain belt, storm tracks, spiral storms, clear deserts) drifting over it and casting shadows, forests, deserts and snow in their own colours, mountains that catch the low sun, turquoise coasts, and the sun glinting on the sea.
- A proper atmosphere: a thin bright limb by day, orange and violet along the edge of night.
- The night side is alive: towns and cities as thousands of small lights, moonlit land and sea, auroras round both poles.
- Stars in their colours, the Milky Way, and the Sun itself in the sky.
- Cables, sea lanes and power lines glow, with light travelling along them; sites ping when you pick them; labels are easier to read.
- The globe now turns and zooms under your hand: the place under the cursor stays under the cursor.

**Space and the ending**
- The solar system view: a living Sun with a corona and flares, the swarm as thousands of sails that catch the light on their orbits, lit planets and asteroids, the Milky Way behind.
- The orbit view: the Earth fills the frame, satellites fly in their planes with trails, stations carry running lights, launches rise as arcs of fire, and a solar storm lights the auroras.
- Reaching Type I is now one unbroken shot: sunrise over the Earth's edge, out past the Moon, and the swarm turning to the Sun.

**The 3D interface**
- A new look for the 3D edition: clean dark glass panels, larger and clearer text, and a K meter that shows your next milestone. (Settings → Display → Interface look → Classic brings the old frames back.)
- A new title screen: the tour fills the screen like a film, with the logo, a simple menu and a caption for each shot. Continue is the first button when you have a save.
- A loading screen with the logo and a progress line.
- The menu's tour is directed: every shot has its own light (golden hour, dusk, night) and its own camera move.
- Bigger screens get a bigger interface on their own; the Steam Deck's screen fits.

**Photo mode**
- On any campus, press Photo: the interface steps aside and the game waits. Choose the hour, the weather, the lens and the blur, and save a 4K picture with no interface in it. Esc goes back.

**Sound**
- A new soundtrack for the 3D edition: seven pieces built on one theme, from the menu through each era to the ending, with warm pads, plucks, bass and gentle drums. The music rests when you pause, picks up when time runs fast and tightens in a finale. The chiptune is still there: Settings → Audio → Soundtrack (it stays the pixel edition's default).
- Every sound effect remade, and new ones for finished buildings, trained models, achievements and act endings. Buttons click, and effects play in the music's key.
- Places sound alive: wind that comes and goes, birds by day, crickets at night, rain with far thunder, surf, the lake, the hush of the Moon. The 3D campuses had no background sound before.
- A proper mix: reverb and echo, a limiter so nothing clips, and stereo. Music is much louder than it was at the same slider (it was very quiet). A separate Ambience volume slider.

**Smoother**
- Medium and low detail do less work per picture than before the new effects went in; high detail no longer draws the scene twice for its shading.

## 0.10.1 — Lived In (2026-11-10)
A big polish pass on the 3D edition: a steady globe, proper roads, real grass and farmland, traffic that drives like traffic, and people with a working day.

**The globe**
- The globe behind the main menu no longer flickers or shimmers as it turns: coastlines, borders, cables and cloud edges are smoothed, and fine detail no longer crawls.
- A richer Earth: the pixel map's colours for every land and the sea's depth bands, relief, sea ice, softer clouds and a clearer highlight on the region you select.
- The solar megaprojects of Acts III–V no longer shimmer either.

**Campuses**
- Roads are one smooth road instead of tiles, with painted lines, stop lines, kerbs, pavements, rounded junctions and a turning circle. The side roads to new buildings get them too.
- No more floating edge: the land is levelled under every plot you could ever build, building never moves the gate or anything already built, and nothing hangs off an edge.
- Grass you can see blade by blade up close, swaying in the wind.
- New rocks, boulders, broadleaf and pine trees near the campus (made with Meshy, like the people), rounder bushes and softer foliage.
- Farmland around the campuses: crop fields in rows, pasture, orchards, irrigation circles in dry country, hedgerows and lines of trees.
- Crisper close-ups, warmer colours in Act I, and daylight you can see by in the far north (Luleå, the fjords, Reykjanes), where the winter sun used to barely clear the horizon.

**Traffic**
- Cars and trucks take real turning arcs, slow down for bends, keep their distance and queue, give way before turning across traffic, and wait at stop lines for a gap. Delivery trucks keep to their lane and unload outside the buildings.

**People**
- People keep a working day on the campus's own clock. Staff arrive in the morning from their cars or the gate and go in by the door; they come out for coffee, phone calls, errands and lunch on the benches; engineers walk the fence line; everyone heads home in the evening. Building crews hammer and carry, and a night guard patrols with a torch.
- Nobody walks through buildings, fences or parked cars any more: people follow the pavements and paths.
- New animations: talking, listening, phone calls, coffee, sitting, hammering, carrying.
- Advisors spend the day where their work is (click one, as before), and Bit floats beside one of them.

**Smoother**
- The 3D resolution steps back up at most every 12 seconds, so the picture doesn't keep sharpening and softening.
- Starting or finishing a building no longer stutters.

## 0.9.4 — Fast Lane (2026-11-08)
Desktop app updates that take seconds instead of half an hour.

**Desktop app updates**
- The game now downloads updates itself: the file comes in small pieces over six connections at once, spread across GitHub's servers. A connection that stalls or crawls is swapped for a fresh one, and nothing it already downloaded is lost. The 93 MB Linux app arrived in 5–7 seconds in tests, where one slow connection had taken over 25 minutes.
- The update is still checked against its signature before anything is installed, exactly as before.
- If this download can't run, the game falls back to the old one.
- This works from the next update on: the update to 0.9.4 itself still uses 0.9.3's download.

## 0.8.1 — Linux sound fix (2026-11-06)

## 0.8.0 — Stakes & Polish (2026-11-06)
A deep pass through the whole campaign before the big playthrough: more fun, clearer, steadier, without new systems. Every finale can now be lost, and every act asks fewer, better questions.

**Your first hour**
- Bit's tutorial is no longer interrupted by event cards, keeps toasts out of the way of what it points at, adds a battery step (solar alone leaves the hall dark at night) and says why a full hall matters.
- Chip orders default to filling the hall, with the free rack space shown — a half-empty first hall used to stretch the Garage to five years or more.
- Goals show progress: "Data Center 31% built · 8 mo to go", "$1.2M / $5M a month", "Bit-2: 45% trained · ~6 mo at today's compute".
- Training runs have beats: a quarter, half and three quarters trained, in Bit's voice. A new model gets a proper toast.
- Penny warns when cash covers less than about six months; build cards show running costs (a research lab is $3.5M a month); the Compute panel shows the next model's ETA before you start it.
- The Research panel only counts what's open to you, shows later techs as locked with their act, and says when there's nothing left to research.

**Fewer, better cards**
- No random cards while a finale is running (the Crunch, the Threshold, the Heat Wall, Solar Maximum).
- Once you're the Steward, company cards stop when your company is in the blind trust, sold or a public utility: the trustee (or its new owners) answers them, in the news.
- Act V draws its own cards, half as often; earlier acts' cards stay in their acts.
- No more cards that charge you for nothing (helium, transformers, export controls), droughts and storms only where you've built, the highlighted choice is always one you can take, and a card with nothing left to choose has a Continue button.

**The Scale-Up (Act II)**
- The Crunch begins once any four of the six IPO goals are met (or by mid-2034), and its tracker explains each front: hover to see what's pushing it and what's holding it back. Your choices matter more; the blockade bites through chips stuck at sea.
- Stuck in phase 1? With Bit-3 trained, Penny finds a smaller Series B after about two and a half years.
- Contract offers show how much you could deliver; export rules are flagged on the site before you build; sovereign deals live on the Blocs screen; board reviews count growth in volume and routine ones are news.
- Fixes: the heat dome no longer hits one region twice, sovereign discounts no longer double up, HBM deals cover what they say, treaties can't be pumped for goodwill.

**The Charter (Act III)**
- The renewal vote judges your decade: the Mandate goals you met, your Legitimacy, how the Threshold went, and whether your own company won the contracts. Sol's count shows every bloc, the ones on the fence and what each outcome means for Act IV; your closing speech can swing a close vote. The Overview shows how the Council would vote today.
- The Threshold builds over its 120 days, and Mandate goals you never met make their front harder.
- Legitimacy falls if the Charter builds nothing for a year or sits on its money, and its tooltip shows what moves it each month.
- Each bid shows what it adds to the monthly payments, with a warning before an award the treasury can't carry; tenders stay open a month after the last bid and you're told when the bids are in.
- Far fewer "a contractor wants more money" cards; Bit's co-pilot stops duplicating contracts and pauses and resumes on its own; a bloc that walks out comes back when it gets what it asked for.
- Phase 3 opens as soon as the Build-Out is done, and the Charter panel says what opens the next phase and by when. Spaceports can be built again in Act IV.

**Planetary (Act IV)**
- Between plans, the planner keeps building by your priorities (Autopilot, on by default; switch it off on Planet → Overview).
- Planet → Overview shows what opens the next plan, with checkmarks and the date it opens anyway. The Fusion Age no longer waits six years for a target it can't reach: Act IV takes about 17–20 years instead of 21–26.
- The planner pays its way: it budgets what it still owes, keeps your reserve, and builds smaller when money is short. A time jump stops early, with its report, if the treasury runs dry, Legitimacy crashes or a bloc walks out.
- The jump report leads with what mattered (fusion pilots, losses, link faults) and shows the treasury; Bit's diary names the pilot results and explains where Legitimacy went.
- The plan screen has "Not yet — let me look first"; Orbit & the Moon can't be picked before stage 3; links, fusion pilots, air capture and water work can be posted during the Mandate.
- The Heat Wall pushes back: its fronts start from the state of your world and rise faster, your choices matter, and a neglected front can be lost.
- Fixes: the tritium shortage can happen, cards that table a resolution are greyed out when it can't be tabled, the World view shows Act IV's target and when growth is capped, and the Goals panel has Act IV's stages.

**Type I (Act V) and the ending**
- Fixed a lock-up when Kip's or Iris's moment came before the longevity treatments were ready.
- The last climb always reaches Kardashev 1.0000 — everyone pushes harder each year — and the shipyards pause for the storm, so Type I always comes after it.
- Solar Maximum is a real test: preparation softens the storm but can't stop it, and a loose strain, minds with no say, low Legitimacy or any Type I checklist item still open push their front. The checklist shows which front each item protects.
- The growth dial shows a forecast Type I year and what each setting risks; every drifting strain gets its card, and the Foundries tab has Halt & verify.
- Going over the Thermal Line no longer sticks; the third era runs up to 10 years and stops by itself at K 0.99; story cards held during an era jump arrive one at a time and say how things are now.
- The ending: Bit's diary of the last climb on the Type I card, the right names and Type II's true scale in the epilogues, the original cast's faces in the credits, your choices in words in the campaign stats, and Bit's final form plus a line about your run on the end card.
- Late Act IV and Act V run three to four times faster.

**Phones and small screens**
- The button bar fits every act (it ran off the screen from Act II on phones and in Act V on laptops), and scrolls with a fade when it must.
- Taps that drift a little still count as taps, and markers are easier to hit with a finger.
- Event cards and panels use the whole screen; buttons Bit points at scroll into view.
- Back (Android) and Escape close menus, dialogs and reports first.

**Smoother everywhere**
- No more slowly growing memory when you hop between the map and close-ups in Acts IV–V (on phones it could end with the app reloading).
- At most 60 frames a second (30 with Battery saver or while the game sits paused): kinder to batteries, and time-lapses take the same time on every screen.

- The Sahara close-up works on phones that can't take very wide images; megaprojects show over the Act IV–V ground tints; the globe's night side has the right colours in every era.

**Time and coming back**
- The clock starts again by itself after a report, a confirm or a card.
- Time away only counts when the game was running and nothing was waiting for you, and not at all in Acts IV–V (the year you reach Type I is your score). Research keeps going while you're away.
- The away report covers the Charter and ends with your next goal.
- Skipping a jump or an era no longer freezes the page.

**Fixes**
- The Hard rescue comes first on the bankruptcy card; emergency cuts cost research; investor terms hold the safety cut for six months.
- A zombie chip order after the Hsinchu quake; two Field Guide entries that couldn't be opened; saves that silently failed in private mode now warn; sounds no longer burst after the tab was hidden; mouse moves over the map no longer redraw the whole interface.

## 0.6.0 — Type I (2026-11-04)
- Act V, Type I: the last act of the campaign, from 2068 to the day Kardashev reaches 1.0000. Your score is the year you get there.
- The Thermal Line: every watt ends up as heat, so the Council caps how much Earth’s surface may use. Almost all of the last hundredfold climb has to be caught and used in space.
- Self-replicating foundries: plant seeds on the Moon, at the L4 and L5 yards and on claimed asteroids, and watch them double. A growth dial trades speed for the chance a strain drifts off-spec.
