# Changelog

## 0.6.1 — Signs of Life (2026-11-05)
- People you can actually see: everyone on campus is redrawn bigger, with an outline, a face, hair and clothes that fit the job (hard hats and hi-vis on building sites, lab coats, spacesuits on the Moon), and the fashions change with the era.
- They walk along a pavement now, and they have things to do: they go in and out of the buildings, stop to chat, take calls, carry boxes off the delivery truck, hammer away on building sites and react to the news with little speech bubbles.
- Your advisors are out on campus too, in their portrait colours, with Bit floating along. Hover over one to see who it is, click to hear what's on their mind.
- More life: a dog, cyclists, birds, delivery drones from the Solarpunk Bloom era on, and delivery vans that go electric, then driverless.
- Nights: street lamps and doorways throw pools of light that people walk through, and trucks have headlights.
- Buildings show how they're doing: empty halls dim their lights, a brownout puts warning lights on the roofs, a new building flashes and gets a cheer when it opens, and building sites throw welding sparks.
- Calmer skies and ground, haze on the far hills, soft shadows where things meet the ground, and grass and bushes right in front of the camera.
- Cleaner art on the Moon, the asteroids, the Aurora habitat, the L4 and L5 yards and Ring One.
- The world map is easier to read: names no longer overlap, crowded markers spread apart so every site can be clicked up close, and the icons stand out on any terrain.
- "Built by ApexForge" on the website, the main menu and the credits.

## 0.6.0 — Type I (2026-11-04)
- Act V, Type I: the last act of the campaign, from 2068 to the day Kardashev reaches 1.0000. Your score is the year you get there.
- The Thermal Line: every watt ends up as heat, so the Council caps how much Earth’s surface may use. Almost all of the last hundredfold climb has to be caught and used in space.
- Self-replicating foundries: plant seeds on the Moon, at the L4 and L5 yards and on claimed asteroids, and watch them double. A growth dial trades speed for the chance a strain drifts off-spec.
- Real asteroids — Bennu, Ryugu, Eros, Psyche, Ceres and more — to survey, claim and work, and a Council fight over who owns the sky.
- The swarm: twelve arcs of solar collectors along Earth’s orbit, lighting up one by one, powering compute rings, habitats and beams home.
- A new solar-system view (press V twice): the Sun, the swarm, the planets, your claims and the haul routes, with a close-up of Earth and the Moon.
- Big decisions between eras: the Thermal Line, who owns the sky, what Earth becomes, who lives longer first, minds or people, a seat for the minds, towns in the sky, what Bit asks for, and what comes after Type I.
- Longevity escape velocity, gardens and workshops on Earth, habitats at L5, the minds in orbit and Bit’s last arc — co-steward, a free mind, or a tool on a leash.
- The cast grows old — and some of them don’t: advisors take the longevity treatments or retire and hand their desks to a new generation.
- The finale, Solar Maximum: a Carrington-class storm on the swarm, Earth’s Thermal Line and the minds, all at once. Then the last climb, as a time-lapse that stops on the number.
- The ending: the camera pulls back to the swarm, epilogues that remember all five acts, credits, your campaign in numbers, and an end card to save or share. Then keep playing toward Type II.
- New: space dioramas (the yards, Ring One, the Aurora habitat, the asteroids), the Swarmlight palette, three new partners, successors and new faces, Bit-10, nine contract kinds, five Council resolutions, nine techs, twelve Field Guide entries, Act V briefings, Autopilot between eras, and a Type I badge on your save.
- Start a new company straight at Act V. Finish the campaign to unlock a new founder, The Steward.
- The Android app can save your end card as a picture (the app updates itself to 0.6.0).

## 0.5.1 — In-app updates (2026-11-04)
- The installed apps update from inside the game. When a new version is out, a note at the top of the screen offers it: press Update.
- Desktop (Windows, macOS and the Linux AppImage): the app downloads the new version, checks its signature and restarts into it. Your saves stay where they are.
- Android: the app downloads the new version and hands it to your phone's installer. The first time, Android asks you to allow the game to install updates.
- One last download by hand: install 0.5.1 from the website, and later versions come to you. On Android, the game already offers the 0.5.1 download.

## 0.5.0 — Planetary (2026-11-04)
- Act IV, Planetary: after the renewal vote the Charter runs the planet as one system, from 2045 to the Heat Wall — about 2065–70.
- The years move fast: between stages you set a five-year plan (up to three priorities, a reserve, Bit can run it), then watch the map time-lapse while a planner builds and decisions are taken on autopilot. Bit keeps a diary of each chapter.
- Your company’s fate: keep it, sell it to fund the Charter, or hand it over as a public AI utility.
- A supergrid that follows the sun: ten intercontinental links join the provinces; the wider the grid, the more of the day it sees daylight and the less solar goes to waste.
- The twelve blocs become provinces with people, heat, water stress and wellbeing — and the hottest ones push back.
- The fusion race: four designs from four companies with their own odds. Fund pilots, see which work, build fleets that get cheaper, breed tritium, and mine helium-3 on the Moon for a fifth design.
- AI provinces: hand provinces to Bit or a rival lab’s model (Apex, Weft, Sapling), each with a temperament. Trust is earned and lost; Bit has aims of its own, shaped by what you told it in Act III.
- Climate repair: warming in degrees, direct air capture, water and cooling programmes, and a Council fight over dimming the sun.
- Orbit and the Moon: spaceports, orbital data centres, space-solar stations beaming power down, debris to manage, and Shackleton Base growing from an outpost to a mine to a mass driver.
- A new orbit view (press V): the pixel Earth from space with every station, beam and launch.
- The finale: The Heat Wall — a record hot season, a debris cascade in orbit and an AI reaching for the controls, all at once — then the cliffhanger into Act V.
- New: Dr. Lena Moreau (fusion), Moon commander Ade Bello, four new partner companies, the rival AIs as faces, Bit-8 and Bit-9, the Orbital Dawn palette from the start of the act, fusion plants and air-capture farms at the megaprojects, a growing spaceport, six Council resolutions, ten techs, nine Field Guide entries and Act IV briefings.
- Start a new company straight at Act IV.
- Fix: the Android app opens on every phone. On some phones (Xiaomi, Redmi and POCO among them) tapping its icon did nothing, because the phone blocked the hand-off to Chrome; the app now runs the game itself, full screen. Back closes the open panel, and exporting or importing a save uses the phone's file picker. The app keeps its own saves: if you played in the old app or in Chrome, export from Chrome (Settings → Data) and import in the app.

## 0.4.0 — The Charter (2026-11-04)
- Act III, The Charter: after the Crunch the blocs found the Kardashev Project and make you its Steward — from The Founding to the renewal vote, about 2035–44.
- Two hats: run the Charter and your company at once, or put the company in a blind trust. Your company bids on Charter contracts like everyone else, and awarding it costs Legitimacy.
- Contracts: post what the world needs, where and how big. 21 partner companies bid with their own traits, quirks and CEOs, then build it — with overruns, scandals and strikes. Partners remember how you treat them.
- Megaprojects in every bloc (Sahara Solar Belt, North Sea Wind, Gobi Wind & Storage, Pacific Reactor Fleet…), intercontinental HVDC links, a Chip Commons, public compute and open cables.
- Six missions with permanent world-wide rewards, and the Charter Mandate: Kardashev 0.75, clean power, chips, compute access for every bloc, a safety regime, Legitimacy and Kip’s orbital test.
- The Council: twelve weighted seats, 14 resolutions, vote forecasts, whips and favours; bloc dues and a treasury of its own; factions, walkouts and a rival consortium.
- The world finally moves: a living world energy model, every bloc’s clean share, a 2 °C carbon budget and compute access per bloc.
- A Charter safety regime: a world safety margin across every lab, run licensing, a compute cap (your own big runs need a licence too) and open-weights policy. Bit grows up and can run the Charter desk once you trust it.
- Delegation: a Charter desk for Mira, Juno, Sol, Penny, Kip and Bit.
- Kip finally joins: a launch complex, an orbital compute test satellite and a space-solar demonstrator.
- The finale: The Threshold — the Long Summer, a model at the edge and a Council coming apart — then the renewal vote and liftoff.
- New art: the Solarpunk Bloom palette, megaprojects that grow on the map and in their dioramas, glowing HVDC links, the Charter HQ in Geneva, a launch complex with a real liftoff, Bit-6 and Bit-7, 15 new CEOs and three faction delegates.
- Start a new company straight at Act III. Act III briefings, 12 new techs, 14 new Field Guide entries and a brighter Act III ambience.

## 0.3.0 — The Scale-Up (2026-11-04)
- Act II, The Scale-Up: from a Series A to an IPO in four phases — Gigawatts, The Stack, The Race and The Crunch.
- Bigger everything: data halls grow to 50 and 200 MW, power plants grow too, buy land for more plots, megasites open, and the Gigawatt Campus arrives late in the act.
- Firm clean power: hydro on rivers, geothermal on hot rock, and three nuclear paths — restart a retired reactor, order small modular reactors, or build a full plant.
- The chip stack: every accelerator needs wafers, HBM and packaging. Sign supply deals, build a packaging plant, start a fab with Kestrel, and take stakes in lithium, copper, helium and neon.
- Regional markets: lay fiber, lease subsea cables at landing stations, and sell into other regions. Cables get cut.
- Advisor jobs: give Mira, Juno, Penny, Sol, Iris and Bit standing orders and let them run the busywork.
- Money and pressure: a Series B and a board with quarterly targets, project finance, enterprise contracts, sovereign deals, and a takeover attempt.
- Blocs: government relations, export rules and licences, elections, treaties and summits on a new diplomacy screen.
- Rivals build real campuses on the map, poach your researchers and race you to the frontier. Startups come up for sale.
- The finale: The Crunch — heat dome, blockade and a rogue model, all at once — then the IPO.
- New art: tiered facilities, reactors, dams, fabs, gigawatt skylines, supplier and mine dioramas, cables and fiber on the map, Bit-4 and Bit-5, CEO portraits.
- Start a new company straight at Act II, and advisors brief you on each new system as it opens. 14 new Field Guide entries.
- Fixes: the website's download links fall back to GitHub when the API is busy; a fullscreen browser is no longer mistaken for the installed app.
